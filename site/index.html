When an AI answer causes harm, who is accountable?

Your agent gives wrong health information or bad financial advice, and someone gets hurt. Was it the model, your data, or the vendor who supplied it? With a record of what the agent retrieved, and from which version of the data, you can show whether the fault was in the source or in what the model did with it.

  • Merkle-DAG storageBlobs, manifests, state and commits are immutable BLAKE3-addressed objects.
  • RFC 6962 checkpoint logEd25519-signable, with O(log n) inclusion and consistency proofs.
  • Compact chunk proofsOne commit plus one state path. Size does not grow with history.
  • Provable audit windowsAnchored bundles reject truncated or re-chained retrieval records.
  • Hybrid retrievalBM25 and HNSW, fused with reciprocal rank fusion.
  • Write-time policyRules reject bad writes, and violations go to a tamper-evident log.
  • Tenant isolationPer-namespace blob salts, per-tenant vector search, scoped BM25 statistics.
  • Subject erasureContent is shredded, hashes stay, and the chain still verifies.
Limits: a proof covers what was retrieved, not whether the answer was correct. A search that skips recording stays invisible. Catching a full history rewrite needs a checkpoint held outside the database. MIT or Apache-2.0.
